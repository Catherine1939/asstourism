Story of Lesotho

Lesotho, officially the Mountain Kingdom, is a small, landlocked country completely surrounded by South Africa. It is the only country in the world whose entire territory lies above 1,000 metres, earning its nickname — Kingdom in the Sky.

The Basotho people were unified under Moshoeshoe I in the early 19th century, who gathered clans at the mountain fortress of Thaba-Bosiu and forged a nation that resisted both Zulu and Boer incursions. Today Lesotho is a constitutional monarchy with a deep cultural identity.

The Basotho blanket — a patterned woollen shawl worn by men and women — and the mokorotlo, the conical woven hat that appears on the national flag, are the country's most recognisable symbols. The Basotho pony, sure-footed on mountain paths, remains a daily mode of transport and the heart of pony trekking.

The official language is Sesotho (with English widely used in government and tourism) and the currency is the Loti (plural Maloti, ISO LSL), pegged 1:1 to the South African Rand.

Timeline of key moments

  1. 1824

    Moshoeshoe I establishes Thaba-Bosiu and unifies the Basotho clans.

  2. 1868

    Basutoland becomes a British protectorate at Moshoeshoe's request.

  3. 1966

    Lesotho gains independence from Britain on 4 October.

  4. 1986

    Katse Dam construction begins under the Lesotho Highlands Water Project.

  5. 2013

    Sehlabathebe National Park inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

  6. 2026

    Bicentenary of the Basotho nation approaches — tourism grows.

Useful travel info

  • Visa: Many nationalities get a free 30-day visa on arrival. Check before travel.
  • Currency: Loti (M), pegged 1:1 with the South African Rand (R accepted).
  • Language: Sesotho & English (official).
  • Best time: Sep–May for hiking; Jun–Aug for snow.
  • Getting there: Fly to Maseru (MSU) or drive from South Africa via Ficksburg/Maseru Bridge.
  • Safety: Generally safe; take normal city precautions in Maseru.
  • Power: 220 V, type M plug (South African three-pin).
